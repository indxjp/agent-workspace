Interview notes with Koji Mikoshiba, team leader in the Maintenance Section (retired March 2026).
Interviewer: Kashiki, Maintenance Section.

## §1 The order to check when pressure will not build

Source: [Veteran note Mikoshiba §1]

First the oil level. Then listen. Then the suction strainer, the relief valve, and last the pump
and coupling, and the unloading valve. Young people want to turn the relief valve's adjusting
screw at once, but that comes last. Once you turn the setting, nobody knows where it was, and you
have two causes instead of one.

## §2 Telling by sound

Source: [Veteran note Mikoshiba §2]

A rattling or grinding sound from the pump means the suction side is losing: low oil or a clogged
suction strainer, causing cavitation. A steady high whine means the relief valve is blowing all
the time. A pump that is oddly quiet with the gauge near zero means the coupling is slipping or
the unloading valve is stuck open.

## §3 Do not turn the relief valve

Source: [Veteran note Mikoshiba §3]

Never raise the relief valve setting because pressure will not build. The AP-200 is set to 21MPa,
and debris caught in it is cured by taking it apart and cleaning it. If you ever touch the setting,
measure it on the gauge and keep a record.

## §4 Winter mornings

Source: [Veteran note Mikoshiba §4]

On winter mornings the oil is cold and stiff, so pressure builds slowly. Run the unit without load
for about ten minutes before judging. While the oil is below 20°C, do not call it a fault.
