# An internal report deck from bullet notes

The company's slide guide (`GUIDE-xx`) decides the layout; where it and this skill differ, the
guide wins. Every bullet that states a fact ends with what it rests on: a row of the figures
(`DATA-01 2026-10`), a plan item (`PRJ-01 M3`) or the notes (`メモ` / `notes`).

1. **Cover** (`表紙` / `Cover`). The deck's name, the department and the date of the talk, from the
   notes. No other bullets.
2. **Conclusion** (`結論` / `Conclusion`). At most three things the talk says, taken from the notes'
   main points, each said in one line.
3. **One slide per message.** Each top-level point of the notes ("why", "what changed") becomes one
   slide whose title states its message as a sentence. Its bullets are the notes' sub-points, made
   plain, at most five; split the slide when there are more.
4. **Figures** (`数字` / `Figures`). The rows the notes ask for, as a `table` copied from the source
   file: the month, the channel and the counts exactly as written, with the file's id and date in the
   slide's last bullet. No total, difference, rate or trend word the file does not support with its
   own figures (say "October's count is 356", not "down 11%").
5. **Plan** (`今後の予定` / `What's next`). Every plan item that is not done, with its planned date
   and its status as the plan states it: a late item keeps its reason and its new date. A date the
   plan has not set is `未定` / `not set`.
6. **Asks** (`お願い` / `Asks`). What the notes ask the audience to do, last.

A slide holds at most 20 bullets and its table at most 15 rows (the `office-writer` limits); the
guide's own limit (five bullets) is lower and applies. No images: `office-writer` writes none.
